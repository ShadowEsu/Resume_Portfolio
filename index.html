03 — RESEARCH

The Research

IN PROGRESS · TARGETING ARXIV + JOURNAL OF STUDENT RESEARCH

Reward Shaping for Accessible AI Tutoring in Community Colleges

Independent researcher · Advised by Prof. Kyu Woong Lee · Diablo Valley College · Jan 2026 — present

Can a rolling window of temporal-difference error act as a dynamic potential function — speeding up Q-learning convergence in sparse-reward environments without a GPU or cloud compute? The paper builds and benchmarks adaptive reward-shaping heuristics grounded in potential-based shaping theory, with AI tutoring for community colleges as the motivating application: tutoring agents that adapt to students without requiring institutional compute budgets.

PIPELINE

Full training and evaluation stack built from scratch in Python — Gymnasium environments, NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib. Vanilla and shaped Q-learning baselines implemented side by side.

RIGOR

Seed-based benchmark runs, convergence plots, statistical significance tests, and ablations across TD-error window sizes W = 10 / 25 / 50 / 100.

FOUNDATION

Self-taught through UC Berkeley CS285 (Deep RL) and Stanford CS234 (RL). Every experiment in the paper runs on consumer hardware — that constraint is the point.

HARDWARE · AI BERKELEY HACKATHON

LifeTap

A physical emergency panic button. One press triggers an 80 dB alarm and sends live GPS coordinates to an emergency contact over WhatsApp as a Google Maps link.

  • Raspberry Pi 3 runs the alert logic in Python, serial-linked to an Arduino handling the GPS module and buzzer in C++ — a complete end-to-end hardware demo, not a slideware concept.
  • Designed for disaster response and personal-safety scenarios where unlocking a phone and opening an app is simply too slow.
  • Packages live GPS coordinates into a ready-to-tap Google Maps link, sent straight to WhatsApp via Twilio.
